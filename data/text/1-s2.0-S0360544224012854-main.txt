1. Introduction
Sponge iron is manufactured in the form of solid by direct reduction
of iron ore. Due to its honeycomb structure, it is called ‘Sponge Iron’.
The sponge iron quality is measured through % metallization, which is
defined in the fraction of metallic iron i.e. Fe to the total iron available in
the final product [1]. This is used in the steel manufacturing process as a
predominant source. Agarwal and Sood [2] found that the required
energy quantity of sponge iron for the kiln is available in the range of
14.63–20.9 GJ/t. A huge heat quantity is produced during the operation,
which is not utilised in the system. So, the problems associated with
energy are the major critical troublesome in this type of industry. Many
investigators suggested numerous ways to the utilisation of waste gas
heat in the process. Bandyopadhyay et al. [3] observed that bulk volatile
matter (VM) quantity was lost with hot waste gases at the kiln feed end.
Elsenheimer and Serbent [4] supported discussed feature and revealed

that waste gas contained around 40 % of total kiln energy. They suggested numerous options to improve energy via the heat of waste gas,
but they didn’t discuss its practical difficulties as well as saved energy
with the installation of these options. Hajidavalloo and Alagheband [5]
incorporated preheating of raw materials using waste gas heat before
admitting to the electric arc furnace. Sponge iron productivity was
enhanced and energy consumption was reduced by 13 % and 14 %.
Eriksson and Larsson [6] proposed different methods to increase process
energy efficiency. These are available through the use of internal energy,
the contribution of external energy and power generation. Mignard and
Pritchard [7] discussed generation of marine energy in remote case and
analyzed transmission and storage in the case of the sponge iron process.
Biswas et al. [8] saved 10–12 % energy considering the reduced amount
of air through axial as well as radial injection.
Kumar and Khanam [9] used waste gas heat for air preheating up to
170◦ C carried out using a plate heat exchanger. As a result, a reduction

2. Description of sponge iron process
The simplified process flow diagram (PFD) of an Indian sponge iron
process, of 500 t/d production capacity, is shown in Fig. 1 along with
industrial data. In this process, raw material admits to the kiln from the
feed end and because of rotation as well as inclination inside the kiln
feed moves to the discharge end. The product collects at the discharge
end. On the other hand, as per process demand air supplies into the kiln
in the form of primary as well as secondary air as indicated in Fig. 1. A
non-oxidising environment is maintained, so that product temperature is
cooled down to 110◦ C from 1,067◦ C. At 110◦ C, solid products come out
from the rotary kiln and enter to rotary cooler [13]. A detailed
description of the process is discussed by Gaurav and Khanam, 2017
[21].

Numerous studies have been done in other plants using WHRB other
than the direct reduced iron (DRI) process. Sui et al. [14] studied energy
as well as exergy efficiencies of the cement production processes.
Without considering WHRB in the process, the values for energy efficiencies for the preparation system of raw material, a rotary system as
well as preparation system of pulverised coal, were obtained as 39.4 %,
50.2 %, as well as 10.8 %. With the installation of WHRB, the energy
efficiencies values for the preparation system of raw material, a rotary
system as well a preparation system of pulverised coal, were predicted as
45.8 %, 55.1 %, and 15.5 %. They found that the saved amount of energy
is 30.75 kWh/t for waste heat power generation, which signified
numerous economic as well as environmental benefits. They also suggested several energy-saving strategies for power generation using waste
heat as a degree of resource, energy-saving effect as well as energy
utilisation. Wang et al. [15] discussed three models based on thermodynamics in detail considering single, dual pressure as well as Energy
Integration systems using Pinch Method principles for energy integration, power generation and heat recovery optimization utilising waste
gas associated with the industry. A Total site hierarchical method
related to energy integration is proposed for increment in power generation added with fuel-saving [16]. Integration based energy system
was most profitable as compared to the other two strategies observed by
them. In the first strategy, heat recovery was carried out the waste gas

3. Description of energy-saving strategy and corresponding
necessary data
A strategy is proposed for saving energy, where preheating of raw
materials as well as power generation are considered simultaneously,
which is a novel option for energy conservation in the sponge iron
process. In this case, preheating of kiln feed, kiln air and slinger coal is
carried out using the heat of waste gas. The remaining heat of waste gas
is used for power generation. For the recovery and utilisation of waste
heat, data on the sponge iron process is collected. The stream data for
this strategy is shown in Table 1 where preheating of feed materials, kiln
air and slinger coal streams are done up to 200 ◦ C, 600 ◦ C and 200 ◦ C.
Waste gas is available from 994 ◦ C to 250 ◦ C. For preheating 9271.99 kW
heat of waste gas is used and beyond that waste gas heat is recovered
through power generation.
Along with stream data, a few more data are required such as specific
heats of various streams and cost data of different materials, which are
reported in Tables 1 and 2. For the installation of a duct to carry waste
gas from one place to another in the process, the distance between

7. Conclusions
(i) The proposed option of Heat Integration in this paper is reckoned
with combined preheating as well as power generation which
accounts for the total waste gas obtainable in the process. Option3 uses total waste gas heat collected from the process, where it
loses heat from 994 ◦ C to 250 ◦ C. The heat exits ABC has been
utilised for Energy Integration. The prominent conclusions are:
On the basis of the payback period combined preheating and
power generation are considered the most cost-effective case as
compared to preheating as well as power generation separately.
In terms of net profits, Option-3 is more cost-effective compared
to the other two options.
(ii) Option-3 is specified as a superlative option in accordance with
heat integration. A valuable amount of coal consumption 8.13 %
is saved which is higher with reference to Kumar work Kumar and
Khanam [9]. Also, an amount of 37.2 % water is saved via integration in case of water consumption.
(iii) The reduction in waste gas is observed and reached 112.8 t/h
from 143.4 t/h. This is a supplementary profit in the case of
Option - 3.
(iv) The existing system utilises 7.45 times higher energy as compared
to the theoretical value. It decreased to 4.69 times due to
appropriate energy integration in the case of Option – 3, it saved
high energy with reference to Prasad work saved 6.2 times only
Prasad et al. [10].
(v) An amount of 2.757 MW of power is generated using waste gas
heat present in the existing system from 994 ◦ C to 250 ◦ C. The
operation of the power generation system is enhanced through
proper solving of problems associated with scaling as well as the
removal of dust that is deposited in boiler tubes and exchangers
too.

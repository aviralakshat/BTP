# 04 Energy balance

**Code:** `project/src/models/energy_balance.py` (`EnergyBalance`)
**Question:** where does the heat in the coal go?

## Purpose

The energy balance is an itemised heat ledger for the kiln. It is what the coal-rate solver closes (model 02), what the axial profile places along the kiln (model 05), and what waste-heat recovery draws on (model 09).

## Basis

The ledger is written against the coal's **net heating value** (NHV), not against a "heat generated by reactions" figure. The NHV is measured, unambiguous and auditable. Reaction heat depends on which reaction set and which volatile-matter surrogate an author chooses. Dey et al. (2015), for instance, model volatile matter as phenol, which cannot be reproduced from a proximate analysis alone.

```
coal NHV in  =  chemical energy leaving unburnt
             +  endothermic process demand (reduction, calcination,
                devolatilisation, moisture evaporation)
             +  sensible heat in every outgoing stream
             +  radiation and shell loss
             +  residual
```

The residual is reported, not distributed.

## The boundary choice

This is the **kiln** boundary, as in Dey et al. (2015). A large share of the coal's volatile matter leaves the kiln unburnt. The model charges its chemical energy as energy crossing the boundary. Dey charges only its sensible heat, because in their accounting the chemical energy is recovered downstream.

That one difference is why closing this ledger calls for about 1.49 t of coal per tonne of DRI on Dey's terms, where Dey measured 1.149. The two balances are drawn around different systems; they do not disagree about the plant. The volatile terms are therefore labelled **to ABC** (sent to the after-burner chamber) rather than "lost", and the waste-heat recovery model claims them back.

## Terms

| Group | Term | How it is computed |
|---|---|---|
| In | Coal net heating value | Feed and injection coal × their NHVs (Dey: 22,530 and 23,362 kJ/kg) |
| In | Sensible heat in ore and coal | Mass × specific heat × (feed temperature − 25 °C); zero at ambient feed |
| Chemical energy | Carbon in char, flue dust and DRI | Lost with the solids |
| Chemical energy | Volatile carbon, volatile hydrogen, CO slip | Sent to the after-burner chamber |
| Process demand | Reduction of iron oxides | Fe₂O₃ to Fe at 824.2 kJ/mol and Fe₂O₃ to FeO at 280.2 kJ/mol, by the product's metallic Fe and FeO |
| Process demand | Dolomite calcination, devolatilisation, moisture evaporation | Standard enthalpies and the devolatilisation heat per kg of volatile matter |
| Sensible heat | DRI, char, free ash, fines, flux, flue dust, waste gas | Mass × specific heat × (exit temperature − 25 °C). Solids leave at 1,020 °C and waste gas at 900 °C |
| Losses | Radiation and shell | 6.76% of coal NHV (Dey Table 11) |

## Result on the reference plant

Specific energy consumption is 5.586 Gcal per tonne of DRI. Shares of coal NHV:

| Term | Share |
|---|---:|
| Reduction of iron oxides | 27.3% |
| Waste-gas sensible heat | 23.1% |
| Volatile carbon to ABC | 21.5% |
| Radiation and shell loss | 6.8% |
| Carbon in dolochar | 5.5% |
| Volatile hydrogen to ABC | 4.5% |
| Carbon in flue dust | 3.0% |
| DRI sensible heat | 3.0% |
| CO slip to ABC | 1.5% |
| Moisture, devolatilisation, calcination, other solids, carbon in DRI | 3.7% combined |
| Residual (solve mode) | under 0.01% |

About 27.5% of the coal's heat leaves the kiln as unburnt fuel for the after-burner. Together with the waste gas's 23.1% sensible heat, this is why sponge-iron waste-heat plants generate as much power as they do.

## Acceptance

The ledger is accepted when the residual is within 12% of coal NHV. That band applies in `specified` mode, where the coal rate is a configured value. In `solve` mode the residual is driven to essentially zero by construction, so the test there is whether the solved coal rate lands in its literature band.

`closure_driver()` reports the unburnt volatile-carbon fraction that would close the ledger at a specified coal rate. It is a diagnostic, not an adjustment.

## Tests

`tests/test_model.py` checks that the ledger closes in solve mode and that the itemised terms sum to the total. `tests/test_hardening.py` checks that every unburnt energy term is labelled either lost or to ABC.

## Limits

The specific heats of DRI and char are engineering defaults that still need a citation (`dri.cp_kj_kgk`, `char.cp_kj_kgk`). Their combined effect is about 3.5% of input heat.
